Mean, median, mode and more

Paste or type your numbers, separated by commas, spaces or new lines, to get the average (mean), median, mode, range, sum, count, minimum and maximum, plus the standard deviation (population and sample), variance and geometric mean.

The formula

Mean = sum ÷ count. For 70, 85, 90 and 95: 340 ÷ 4 = 85. The median is the middle value once the numbers are sorted (87.5 here), and the mode is the value that appears most often.

Grades and percentages

Enter your marks or percentages to see your mean score. If assignments carry different weights, use the Grade Calculator; for a grade point average, use the GPA Calculator.

Average buy price of shares

When you buy the same stock at different prices, the simple average is wrong — you need a weighted average: total cost ÷ total shares. 10 shares at $50 plus 30 at $40 → $1,700 ÷ 40 = $42.50, not $45.

Other everyday averages

  • Fuel economy of a car or bike: kilometres driven ÷ litres used.
  • Times: convert minutes and seconds to decimals before averaging.
  • Batting average in baseball: hits ÷ at-bats.
  • Average speed over a route: total distance ÷ total time.

In Excel or Google Sheets, the formula is =AVERAGE(A1:A10).

Worked examples

The numbers already in the box (12, 15, 18, 22, 22, 30, 41) add up to 160. Divide by 7 and the mean is about 22.86. The median is 22, the middle value of the sorted list, and the mode is also 22 because it appears twice. The range is 41 − 12 = 29.

When the mean misleads. Five salaries of 30k, 32k, 35k, 38k and 250k have a mean of 77k, yet four of the five people earn far less. The median, 35k, describes the group much better. Use the median whenever one or two values are far from the rest, as with house prices, incomes or delivery times.

Averaging percentages. Scoring 45/50 (90%) on one test and 60/100 (60%) on another does not give you 75% overall. Add the marks instead: 105 out of 150 is 70%. Only average the percentages directly when every test is out of the same total or counts equally.

Weighted average. If one value counts more than another, multiply each value by its weight first. Marks of 80 with weight 2 and 90 with weight 3 give (160 + 270) ÷ 5 = 86, not 85.

Sample or population standard deviation?

The calculator shows both. Use the population figure when your list contains every value you care about, such as all 30 students in one class. Use the sample figure when your numbers are a sample of a bigger group, such as 20 people from a survey of a whole city. The sample version divides by n − 1 instead of n, which makes it slightly larger.

Tip: you can paste a column straight from Excel or Google Sheets, since new lines work as separators. Remove thousands separators first, because a comma also separates numbers here, so 1,250 would be read as 1 and 250.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find the average of percentages?

If every percentage comes from the same total, add them and divide by how many there are. If the totals differ, add the raw marks and divide by the combined total instead, otherwise the result is wrong.

Is the average the same as the mean?

In everyday use, yes. "Average" usually means the arithmetic mean: the sum divided by the count. Median and mode are other kinds of average, and this page shows all three.

Can I paste numbers from Excel?

Yes. Copy a column and paste it into the box, since new lines separate the numbers. Remove thousands separators such as 1,250 first, because commas also act as separators.
