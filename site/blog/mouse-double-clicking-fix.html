Mouse Double Clicking on a Single Click? How to Test and Fix It

Quick answer: a mouse that double clicks on a single click almost always has a worn button switch. Confirm it with a free mouse double click test, then try: checking Windows mouse settings, updating the mouse software, discharging static, and cleaning. If the test still shows double clicks, replace the switch or the mouse — or use the warranty.

Files open when you meant to select them, drag-and-drop lets go halfway, and games register two shots instead of one. Mouse double clicking is one of the most common computer faults — and it can happen to cheap and expensive mice alike. Here's how to confirm the problem and fix it.

Why mice start double clicking

Under each mouse button is a small microswitch: a thin metal spring that snaps against a contact when you press. Over hundreds of thousands of clicks, the metal tires and oxidises. Instead of making one clean contact, it "bounces" and sends two clicks a few milliseconds apart. Other causes include:

  • Dust or debris inside the switch.
  • Static build-up in wireless mice.
  • Mouse software or driver settings (for example a very low "debounce time" on some gaming mice).
  • Windows settings that make normal clicking behave unexpectedly.

Step 1: Test your mouse for double clicking

  1. Open the Mouse Double Click Test.
  2. Click normally inside the box — single clicks, at your usual speed — 50 to 100 times. Faults often appear only after many clicks.
  3. Test the right, middle (wheel) and side buttons too, and scroll the wheel both ways.
  4. Read the result. Any two presses closer together than the threshold (80 ms by default) are flagged as a double click, and the table shows which button is affected.

A real human double-click is normally well over 80 ms apart, so a flagged click is almost certainly the hardware. If you get a few false alarms because you click very fast, switch the threshold to 50 ms. The click log shows the exact gap between every click.

Step 2: Rule out settings and software

  • File Explorer click setting (Windows): open File Explorer options and check Click items as follows. "Single-click to open" can feel like a double click, but it isn't a fault.
  • Double-click speed: Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Mouse → Additional mouse settings → Buttons tab. A very slow setting can make two separate clicks count as a double click.
  • ClickLock: on the same Buttons tab, make sure ClickLock is off. It holds the button down after a long press, which makes dragging behave strangely.
  • Mouse software: update Logitech, Razer, SteelSeries or other mouse software and firmware. Some gaming mice have a debounce time setting — raising it slightly can hide a worn switch.
  • Driver: in Device Manager, expand Mice and other pointing devices, uninstall the mouse and restart.
  • Try another computer or USB port. If the mouse double clicks everywhere, the problem is in the mouse.

Step 3: Quick hardware fixes to try

  1. Discharge static (wireless mice): switch the mouse off, remove the battery, then hold down both buttons for about 30 seconds. Put the battery back and test again.
  2. Blow out dust: use short bursts of compressed air into the gaps around the button while clicking it.
  3. Click firmly a few dozen times: sometimes this temporarily clears oxidation from the switch contacts. It's a short-term fix at best.

After each step, run the double click test again to see if anything changed.

Step 4: Permanent fixes

  • Warranty: if the mouse is still under warranty, contact the manufacturer — double clicking is a well-known fault and is usually covered.
  • Replace the switch: with a soldering iron, you can replace the worn microswitch with a new one, often for a few dollars. Many tutorials exist for popular mice; it takes 20–30 minutes if you're comfortable with soldering.
  • Swap buttons temporarily: in Windows mouse settings you can swap the primary and secondary buttons to keep working while you wait for a repair.
  • Replace the mouse: if you buy a new one, look for models with optical switches, which use light instead of a metal contact and don't suffer from this kind of bounce.

Double clicking in games

In games, a bouncing switch causes double shots, accidental item drops and failed drag-clicks. If you also want to check how fast you can click, the CPS test measures clicks per second — but run the double-click test first, because a faulty switch inflates CPS scores with clicks you never made.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my mouse double click when I click once?

Almost always because the small switch under the button is wearing out. Its metal contact bounces and sends two clicks instead of one. Less often, it's caused by a Windows setting, a driver problem or dirt inside the switch.

Can a double-clicking mouse be fixed?

Sometimes. Settings and driver problems are easy to fix, and a static discharge or cleaning occasionally helps. If the switch itself is worn, the reliable fixes are replacing the switch (soldering) or replacing the mouse.

How can I tell if my mouse is double clicking?

Click normally in a mouse double-click test about 50–100 times. It flags any two clicks closer together than a human can click — usually under 80 milliseconds — and tells you which button is faulty.

Is mouse double clicking covered by warranty?

Usually yes, if the mouse is still within its warranty period. Double-clicking from a worn switch is a common and well-known fault.

How do I change my mouse to open files with a single click?

In Windows, open File Explorer options and under "Click items as follows" choose single-click or double-click. This is a setting, not a fault, so it doesn't cause random double clicks.
