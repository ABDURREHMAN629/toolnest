How the Hijri calendar works

The Islamic (Hijri) calendar is lunar. Each of its 12 months begins with the new crescent moon and lasts 29 or 30 days, so a Hijri year is about 354 days — 11 days shorter than the Gregorian year. That is why Ramadan and Eid move about 11 days earlier every year. Years are counted from the Hijrah of the Prophet ﷺ to Madinah in 622 CE and are written with "AH".

Umm al-Qura and moon sighting

This converter uses the Umm al-Qura calendar, the official calendar of Saudi Arabia, which is calculated in advance. Many countries — including Pakistan, India and Bangladesh — begin each month after the moon is actually sighted locally, so their date is often one day behind Saudi Arabia. Use the moon-sighting adjustment to match your local calendar, and follow your local moon-sighting committee for the start of Ramadan and the Eids.

Examples

  • 1 Muharram 1446 AH (Islamic New Year) = 7 July 2024
  • 1 Ramadan 1446 AH = 1 March 2025
  • 1 Shawwal 1446 AH (Eid al-Fitr) = 30 March 2025
  • 1 Ramadan 1447 AH = 18 February 2026 (Umm al-Qura)

The 12 Islamic months

Muharram, Safar, Rabi al-Awwal, Rabi al-Thani, Jumada al-Ula, Jumada al-Akhirah, Rajab, Sha'ban, Ramadan, Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'dah and Dhu al-Hijjah.
