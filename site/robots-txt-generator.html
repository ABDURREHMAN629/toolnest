What is robots.txt?

It is a plain text file at the root of your site that tells crawlers which pages they may visit. Before crawling, a bot reads yoursite.com/robots.txt and follows the rules for its user-agent. It must sit at the root of the domain; it won't work in a sub-folder.

Example

User-agent: *
Disallow: /admin/
Allow: /admin/help/

Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml

Disallow: / blocks the whole site, and an empty Disallow: allows everything. Google ignores Crawl-delay; Bing respects it.

AI crawlers

The AI option adds a Disallow: / group for each AI crawler. Be aware that it also blocks AI search bots such as OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, so your pages may stop appearing in AI answers — remove those lines if you want that traffic.

Blogger, WordPress and Cloudflare

  • Blogger — Settings → Crawlers and indexing → turn on “Enable custom robots.txt”, then paste. Keep Disallow: /search so label pages aren't indexed.
  • WordPress serves a virtual file; edit it with your SEO plugin's file editor, or upload a real file to the site root.
  • Cloudflare can add AI-bot rules to your robots.txt automatically; check the result after turning it on.

Do you need one?

Not strictly — without one, everything may be crawled — but it's good practice for the sitemap line. It isn't security or a legal barrier: it's a request that well-behaved bots follow. To keep a page out of Google, use a noindex tag instead. Never block CSS, JavaScript or pages you want to rank, and check your file with Search Console's robots.txt report.

Ready-made files for common sites

WordPress. Keep the admin area out of crawls but leave the AJAX file open, because themes and plugins load content through it:

User-agent: *
Disallow: /wp-admin/
Allow: /wp-admin/admin-ajax.php

Sitemap: https://example.com/wp-sitemap.xml

WordPress has served /wp-sitemap.xml since version 5.5. If you use Yoast or Rank Math, point the Sitemap line at /sitemap_index.xml instead.

Online shop. Block cart, checkout and account pages, plus filtered URLs that create thousands of near-identical pages: Disallow: /cart, Disallow: /checkout, Disallow: /*?sort=. Shopify generates its own file; to change it you edit the robots.txt.liquid template in your theme.

Staging site. Disallow: / keeps a test copy out of crawls, but remove it the day you launch. Forgetting this line is one of the most common reasons a new site never appears in Google.

How Google reads your rules

  • The most specific rule wins. With Disallow: /admin/ and Allow: /admin/help/, the longer Allow rule applies to the help pages. If two rules are equally long, Google uses Allow.
  • Wildcards. * matches any characters and $ marks the end of the URL, so Disallow: /*.pdf$ blocks every PDF.
  • Paths are case-sensitive. /Admin/ and /admin/ are different rules.
  • Each subdomain needs its own file. shop.example.com does not use the rules at www.example.com.
  • Changes take a little time. Google usually caches robots.txt for up to a day, so a fix is not seen instantly.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I upload robots.txt?

In the root folder of your domain, so it opens at https://yourdomain.com/robots.txt. A file in a sub-folder such as /blog/robots.txt is ignored by crawlers.

Will robots.txt remove a page from Google?

No. A blocked page can still be indexed without its content if other sites link to it. To remove a page, let Google crawl it and add a noindex meta tag, or use the Removals tool in Search Console.

Should I block AI crawlers?

Only if you do not want your content used for AI training. Blocking is a choice, not an SEO requirement. Remember that blocking AI search bots can also stop your pages appearing in AI answers.
